# Showing up on forums and communities (safely)

The aim is to be the helpful person who happens to build websites, not an advert. Done properly this earns trust, referrals and the occasional link. Done badly it gets accounts banned and the brand a poor name.

## Ground rules

1. **Say who you are.** Use a profile that names OneSimpleSite or a real person from it. Never use fake accounts, sock puppets or pretend to be a happy customer. Never ask friends to post fake praise. That is against every platform's rules and the UK's consumer rules on fake reviews.
2. **Read the group's rules first.** Many ban promotion outright. Ask a moderator before posting anything promotional.
3. **Help first.** Answer the question asked, fully, without a link. A link is fine only when it truly answers the question and the rules allow it.
4. **Be honest about competitors.** Speak about GoDaddy, One.com, Wix, Squarespace or WordPress only in factual, fair terms, as the guides do. No digs, no claims you cannot back up. Say when a builder is the right choice.
5. **No private data.** Do not paste anyone's details, and do not discuss a customer without their written permission.
6. **No spam and no scheduling tools that look like it.** A few useful replies a week beats a burst.

## Where

- Facebook groups for local businesses and trades in the places on the local pages (Hertfordshire, Bedfordshire, Buckinghamshire, London). Join as the business page or as yourself, and follow the group's promotion day if it has one.
- Reddit communities about small business and web design (read the sidebar rules; most limit self-promotion to a small share of your activity).
- Q&A and small-business forums where people ask "builder or designer?", "how much should a website cost?", "how do I get on Google?".
- Local business networks and chamber of commerce groups, where being known matters more than links.
- LinkedIn and the other social accounts (@onesimplesite): short practical posts built from the guides.

## What to post (all of it true and useful)

- Plain answers to common questions, adapted from the guides: renewal prices to check, owning your domain, what local search needs, what a fixed-price package covers.
- Before and after explanations of a real change, only with the customer's permission.
- A short checklist people can save, such as "5 things every trades website needs on the first screen".
- Honest "this isn't for you" advice. It builds more trust than any pitch.

## Pictures

Use real screenshots of sites you built and have permission to show, or simple diagrams made for the post. Avoid stock photos of people at laptops and avoid generated images that pretend to be real customers or real work.

## Writing style

Write the way you would explain it to a customer in person: short sentences, specific examples, no "in today's digital landscape", no lists of three adjectives, no exclamation marks. If a sentence could appear on any website, cut it.

## Tracking

Keep a simple log: where you posted, the rule you checked, what was asked, and whether it led to anything. Stop posting anywhere it brings nothing after a couple of months.
