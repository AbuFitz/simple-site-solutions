# OneSimpleSite research notes

All sources accessed 2026-10-14. "Says" = what the source states (paraphrased). "Inference" = my reading, not a source claim. Competitor pages are self-published vendor claims, not verified.
Not found: a primary FSB / Which? / Google survey on what small businesses want from a website. Section 1 therefore leans on DSIT-derived secondary data and vendor guides. Treat as indicative.

## 1. What UK small-business owners look for when buying a website

**Says**
- A third-party stats site, citing the DSIT UK Business Data Survey 2026 (published 18 Jun 2026), reports 78% of UK businesses have a website, up from 68% in 2023-24. It gives 92% for small (10-49 employees), 84% for micro (1-9) and 76% for sole traders. I could not confirm these on gov.uk; the 2026 release page I retrieved had no website figures. [https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-website-statistics/] [https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/uk-business-data-survey-2026]
- The same roundup says the top reasons for having no site were "not relevant to my industry" (about 27%) and cost (26%). Secondary and unverified. [https://weblane.co.uk/small-business-website-statistics/]
- Vendor cost guides give a UK small-business build as roughly £1,000-£6,000, with first-year total cost of ownership £1,800-£8,000. Another guide puts hosting at £8-£100/month. These are agency estimates. [https://www.baslondigital.com/post/costs-of-website]
- A UK agency guide warns that "free hosting" is usually the first year only, and that buyers should budget for domain renewal and hosting. [https://www.webfactory.co.uk/blog-articles/launching-your-new-website---part-2]
- A UK guide advises that a managed monthly website is a service, not automatically a transferable asset. It also warns of owners locked out of their own domain or files held in a provider's account. [https://www.baslondigital.com/post/costs-of-website]
- A contract-trap guide advises capping terms at 12 months, getting code, design files, domain and content ownership in writing, and checking VAT treatment. [https://redefineweb.com/blog/pay-monthly-websites-uk-plans-real-pricing-contract-traps/]
- GOV.UK says a limited company's website must show its registration number, registered office address, the UK nation of registration and "Limited"/"Ltd". [https://www.gov.uk/running-a-limited-company/signs-stationery-and-promotional-material]

**Inference (not source claims)**
- The recurring buyer questions look like: total cost in year 1 and year 2+, who owns the domain and files, can I leave and take the site, what is excluded (copy, photos, extra pages, changes), how long it takes, and is this a real, contactable company.
- OneSimpleSite should show: all-in price including VAT treatment, a "what you own" statement, a plain £29/month hosting explainer (Hosting & Care) with exit terms, an included/excluded table, and company details in the footer.

## 3. Studio / productised-service references (patterns only)

- **Designjoy** [https://www.designjoy.co/]. Says: one flat monthly plan, a three-step process, risk-reducers beside the price (pause or cancel), and an FAQ accordion covering turnaround, revisions, exclusions and refunds. Inference: put objections in the FAQ and reassurance next to the price. Its crossed-out "discount" price is a pattern to avoid for a trust-first brand.
- **StudioMeyer pricing** [https://studiomeyer.io/en/webdesign/preise]. Says: three tiers with the middle one marked recommended, delivery time per tier, an explicit "not included" list, prices ex-VAT, ownership of code and content once paid in full, a care plan free for 12 months then a stated monthly price, and what happens to data after cancelling (14 days online, 6 months export). Inference: the strongest model of ownership and exit honesty.
- **Websites for Tradespeople** [https://www.websitesfortradespeople.co.uk/]. Says: fixed price in writing, handover note, results reported as enquiries rather than rankings, and delays usually come from the client supplying content. Inference: copy the honest "what slows a launch" framing.
- **Mapletree Studio** [https://mapletree.studio/72-hour-package/]. Says: a short intake form, a reply promise within one working day, a 3-step "what happens next", and Companies House details. It shows no prices. Inference: the next-step clarity is worth borrowing; hiding price is not.
- **Clearleft work index** [https://clearleft.com/work]. Says: cards use a sector tag, client logo, an outcome-style headline and a one-line challenge question. The index shows no metrics. Inference: lead each case study with the problem. State what was measured and what was not. Label demo concepts as demos.
- **ManyPixels package roundup** [https://www.manypixels.co/blog/web-design/website-design-packages]. Says: tiers scale by page count and add services at higher tiers; "from" prices can differ from the real cost once add-ons are included. The article is self-promotional. Inference: use fixed prices, not "from".

## 4. UK legal / regulatory basics (flag for owner / legal review)

**Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 (14-day cancellation)**
- Reg. 4 says a consumer is "an individual acting for purposes which are wholly or mainly outside that individual's trade, business, craft or profession", and a trader acts for purposes relating to their trade or business. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/4]
- GOV.UK's distance-selling page says customers may cancel up to 14 days after delivery without giving a reason. If the seller never tells them of the right, the cancellation window can run up to 12 months. It does not say whether the rules cover business buyers. [https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/consumer-contracts-regulations]
- Reg. 36 says a trader must not start a service before the cancellation period ends unless the consumer expressly asks it to. If they cancel after it has started, they pay a proportionate amount. No payment is due if the required information was not given or early start was not requested. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36]
- Business Companion (via search snippet) says the Regulations cover trader-to-consumer contracts and that occasional sales may not count as an organised distance scheme. I could not fetch the page. [https://www.businesscompanion.info/consumer-contracts-distance-sales]
- Inference: a sole trader buying a website for their business is probably acting for business purposes and so probably is not a "consumer". Mixed or personal purchases are a grey area. The Consumer Rights Act definition covers "wholly or mainly" outside trade, and one commentary says the seller bears the burden of proving someone is not a consumer. [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/2/notes]
- **Review flag:** decide whether to (a) state "for business customers only" with a purchaser declaration at checkout, and/or (b) voluntarily offer a refund/cancellation window. If any consumers might buy, get the reg. 36 early-start consent wording and cancellation information checked by a solicitor.
- **Review flag:** limited-company details in the footer (number, registered office, nation of registration, "Ltd") are required on websites. [https://www.gov.uk/running-a-limited-company/signs-stationery-and-promotional-material]

**ICO / PECR cookies**
- ICO says guidance on storage and access technologies was published 20 Dec 2024 and last updated 29 Apr 2026 (finalised). [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/]
- Its older cookies page says consent needs "some form of unambiguous positive action" and that continuing to browse is not consent. It also says non-essential cookies cannot be set on the homepage before consent. [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/]
- Exceptions page: "strictly necessary" is judged from the user's point of view, and no advertising purpose qualifies. The statistical-purposes exception requires aggregate use only for improving the service, no tracking of individuals, limited sharing, and clear information plus a simple, free way to object. [https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/]
- Vendor blogs say Data (Use and Access) Act changes to PECR were commenced 5 Feb 2026 and raised fines to up to £17.5m or 4% of turnover. I did not verify this on official pages. [https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/uk-data-use-and-access-act-2025-pecr-cookie-consent-changes-and-how-to-comply]
- Inference: a site that sets no cookies beyond strictly necessary ones (or uses cookieless, aggregate analytics that meets the exception) needs no consent banner, but still needs a clear cookie/privacy notice. Any ad pixels, session recording or marketing tags need prior consent.
- **Review flag:** the privacy policy, the analytics choice and the banner decision.

**Stripe Checkout (hosted page), UK**
- Lifecycle per Stripe: the server creates a Checkout Session, redirects the customer to the Stripe-hosted URL, then a webhook on `checkout.session.completed` fulfils the order. A session expires after 24 hours by default (30 minutes to 24 hours configurable). [https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/how-checkout-works.md?payment-ui=stripe-hosted]
- Test mode: sandboxes use test API keys and test cards and make no real charges. Live keys belong in environment variables or a secrets vault, not in source. The legacy "test mode sandbox" shares some settings with live mode. [https://docs.stripe.com/test-mode]
- Webhook signatures: verify the `Stripe-Signature` header with the `whsec_` secret using the official library. The raw request body must not be altered. The default timestamp tolerance is 5 minutes (never 0). Return 2xx quickly. [https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks#verify-events]
- Duplicate events: log processed event IDs. Event order is not guaranteed. [https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks#verify-events]
- Fulfilment: webhooks are required, because customers may never reach the success page. Fulfilment must run once per Checkout Session even if called repeatedly or concurrently, and should check `payment_status` and record the result. [https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment.md?payment-ui=stripe-hosted]
- Idempotency: send an `Idempotency-Key` (up to 255 characters, V4 UUID suggested, no personal data) on POST requests. Stripe returns the saved response for the same key, and API v1 keys can be pruned after 24 hours. [https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests]
- Server-side prices: Stripe's docs show `line_items` using a server-held Price ID or `price_data`. I found no official page saying "never trust client prices". A third-party write-up recommends the browser send only a plan key, with the server mapping it to a Price ID from an allowlist. [https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/how-checkout-works.md?payment-ui=stripe-hosted] [https://lilting.ch/en/articles/stripe-price-server-side]
- Stripe's security guide says payment pages need TLS 1.2 or above, webhooks should use TLS, and signature verification should be combined with IP allowlisting. [https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide]
- Inference for the build: client sends only package id (launch/growth) and the optional hosting flag; the server maps to Price IDs; one session per order with an idempotency key; fulfil only from a signature-verified webhook.
- **Review flag:** VAT status and whether to collect tax, receipts and invoices, terms acceptance at checkout, and how the £29/month subscription is cancelled (the Checkout `mode` and the customer portal).

## 5. Performance and accessibility thresholds

- Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds at the 75th percentile, segmented by mobile and desktop: LCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or less. [https://web.dev/articles/vitals]
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA: pointer targets are at least 24 by 24 CSS px, with exceptions for spacing, an equivalent control, inline text, user-agent controls and essential presentation. [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html]
- SC 2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced) is Level AAA at 44 by 44 CSS px. [https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html] The brief's 44px target is therefore a stricter internal standard than the AA requirement. This is an inference.
- Other new WCAG 2.2 items relevant to forms: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum, AA, e.g. sticky banners), 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA), 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A), 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum, AA). [https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/]
